// 07. Why These Colors

Violet sits between red (human warmth) and blue (technology). It is the Magitek fusion — intelligence fused with the machine, not bolted on. Historically the dye of sovereignty: expensive, rare, reserved for rulers. Not aggressive like red. Not obedient like blue. The color of authority that doesn't need to raise its voice.

Warm bone reads as parchment, vellum, classified document. The Captain's desk. Not sterile white (corporate, institutional) or cool gray (generic SaaS). Warm enough to feel human. Old enough to feel authoritative. The paper a Manifest would be written on if it were physical.

Violet-black is the dark mode insight: the accent hue becomes the substrate. You're not reading a dossier anymore — you're inside the machine. The intelligence isn't painted on the surface; it is the environment. This mirrors the Esver philosophy: the AI isn't an app you open, it's the OS you inhabit.

Warm off-white text (#EAE7E3) carries the bone's humanity into dark mode. The violet lives in the environment (bg, borders, accents), but the words stay warm — because a human wrote them. Even inside the machine, the Captain's voice is grounded, not ethereal.

The peak state exists because the brand has an arc. "Most people accept tools that watch them" is calm observation. "Awaken yours" is a moment of commitment. The peak accent (#9D8FD6 light / #B8ABE6 dark) is not louder — it is focused. Used only where the whisper becomes a declaration: CTA buttons, the boot sequence, the awakening. Restraint makes it matter.

The principle: In light mode, warmth connects surface to accent. In dark mode, the accent hue itself connects surface to accent. Both modes feel like the same brand because violet isn't decoration. It's the atmosphere sovereignty lives in.
